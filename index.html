A short story about building with AI

You built it with AI. Do you know what’s inside?

Panya is an advisor that works inside the AI tools you already use.

It’s a learning companion for people new to building with AI. It helps you notice the decisions your AI tool makes along the way, understand what they mean, and develop your own sense of what’s right for what you create.

Chapter one

You build something with AI.

Imagine you’re a teacher, and you want an app to track your students’ reading. You describe it, and in minutes there it is. It works on the first try. It feels a little like magic.

Chapter two

But beneath the surface, choices were made.

AI tools make many choices while they build. Most are routine. Some aren’t, and the app looks finished either way.

Can you spot the choice that matters here?

There it is: your students’ names and notes were stored on an online server so the app could sync. Nobody asked you.

Chapter three

That’s when a curious drop turns up.

You ask Panya, “What am I missing?” It doesn’t take over. It wonders, out loud, with you.

“Your app keeps students’ names and reading notes on an online server, not just on your laptop. That makes syncing possible, but it decides who could see their information. Want to think it through, or keep it as is for now?”

What would you do?

You

Let’s think. I only use it on my school laptop. I just want to see who needs extra help.

Panya

Then keeping everything on your laptop fits, and students’ names never leave it. The tradeoff: nothing is backed up, so a lost laptop means lost notes. Your school may also have rules about student data, which are worth checking. Whether the tradeoff is OK is your call.

How would you feel about losing the notes?

You

I’d rather lose notes than risk my students’ privacy. I’ll export a copy each month.

I can’t lose these notes. I need them backed up.

Panya

Then an online copy fits, as long as only you can open it. That means adding a sign-in, and it’s worth checking your school’s rules before students’ names go online. That’s a reasonable call, and it’s yours.

your decision

  • Your reasoning: “I can’t lose these notes”
  • Accepted risk: students’ names are stored outside my devices
  • Check it works: open the app in a private window and confirm you can’t see the notes without signing in

Panya

Chapter four

You decide.

Whatever you chose, the call was yours, and so was the reasoning. Panya’s job was to make sure you saw the choice, understood the tradeoff, and had a way to check the result.

Your decision card: notes stay on my laptop, my students’ privacy first, and I accept no automatic backup.

Chapter five

Next time, you spot it first.

A new app, a new choice, and this time you’re the one asking, “Who can see this?” Panya just smiles. Its support steps back as your judgment grows. It was always meant to be a mentor you outgrow.

Common questions

How is this different from the AI tools I already use?

Panya isn’t a separate tool. It’s an advisory mode that runs inside them, such as Claude, Claude Code, Cursor, or VS Code. Those tools focus on output; Panya focuses on your judgment. It won’t make consequential choices for you, and it labels what it has verified versus what it’s guessing.

Will this slow me down?

Only where it counts. Panya stays quiet on routine work, and when it speaks up you can skip the conversation and go with a default it tells you about. The exception is a serious risk, like losing data or exposing someone’s private information: Panya always says so plainly, and the call is still yours.

What does it save about me?

Only with your permission, Panya keeps a log of your decisions and a short profile of which topics you know well and how you like things explained, as files in your own project. You can read, edit, or delete them at any time.
