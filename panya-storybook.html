A short story about building with AI

AI can build it. Only you can decide if it’s right.

Panya is a learning companion for people new to building with AI. It helps you notice the decisions AI makes along the way, understand what they mean, and develop your own sense of what’s right for what you create.

We’ll only email you when Panya is ready to try.

Chapter one

You build something with AI.

You’re a teacher, and you want an app to track your students’ reading. You describe it, and in minutes there it is. It works on the first try. It feels a little like magic.

Chapter two

But beneath the surface, choices were made.

AI tools make many choices while they build. Most are routine. Some quietly decide who can see your data, or what happens when something goes wrong, and the app looks finished either way.

Here’s how it happens

  1. You ask

    “Build an app to track my students’ reading progress.”

  2. Your AI builds it

    “Done! Your app is ready to use.” It looks great and works on the first try.

  3. What it quietly decided

    Your students’ names and notes are stored on an online server so the app can sync. Nobody asked you.

  4. You ask Panya “what am I missing?”

    Panya points out where your students’ information went, and offers to think it through with you.

Chapter three

That’s when a curious drop turns up.

Panya doesn’t take over. It runs inside the AI tool you build with. Ask it anytime, and it also speaks up when a choice is hard to undo. Your standards, your call, and a way to check the result.

Advice at the moment it matters

You

What am I missing?

Panya

Your app keeps students’ names and reading notes on an online server, not just on your laptop. That makes syncing possible, but it decides who could see their information.

Want to think it through, or keep it as is for now?

You

Let’s think. I only use it on my school laptop. I just want to see who needs extra help.

Panya

Then keeping everything on your laptop fits, and students’ names never leave it. The tradeoff: nothing is backed up, so a lost laptop means lost notes. Your school may also have rules about student data, which are worth checking. Whether the tradeoff is OK is your call.

How would you feel about losing the notes?

You

I’d rather lose notes than risk my students’ privacy. I’ll export a copy each month.

your decision

  • Decision: keep student notes on my laptop only
  • Your reasoning: “I’d rather lose notes than risk my students’ privacy”
  • Accepted risk: notes added since the last monthly export are lost if the laptop is
  • Check it works: turn off Wi-Fi, add a note, reopen the app, and confirm it’s still there

Chapter four

You decide.

AI tools are quick to say “done.” Panya slows down at the moments that matter, so you learn to tell the difference between an app that looks finished and one that’s right for you.

An advisor, not an autopilot

It speaks up before big choices

When a choice is hard to undo, like where your data lives, who can see what, or adding a paid service, Panya names it in a sentence and offers to think it through. Skip it, and it goes with a sensible default it told you about.

Real expertise, honestly labeled

Panya brings engineering, design, and privacy know-how, explained in terms of your app, not just questions. It separates what it has verified from what it’s guessing, and never claims a test passed unless one actually ran.

You make the call

Your goals and your standards decide what “good” means. Panya offers advice and evidence, then leaves the decision with you.

Chapter five

Next time, you spot it first.

A new app, a new choice, and this time you’re the one asking the question. Panya just smiles. Its support steps back as your judgment grows, and it was always meant to be a mentor you outgrow.

Chapter six

The wisdom is yours.

Panya (ปัญญา) means wisdom in Thai. Not the AI’s wisdom. Yours, growing a little with every decision you make.

You don’t have to become a programmer

Just understand enough to make the calls that matter: where your data lives, who can see it, and what happens if it’s lost. Panya builds on what you already know and fills in what you don’t.

Common questions

How is this different from the AI tools I already use?

Panya isn’t a separate tool. It’s an advisory mode that runs inside them, such as Claude, Claude Code, Cursor, or VS Code. Those tools focus on output; Panya focuses on your judgment. It won’t make consequential choices for you, it labels what it has verified versus guessed, and it steps back as your judgment grows.

Will this slow me down?

Only where it counts. Panya stays quiet on routine work, and when it speaks up you can skip the conversation and go with a default it tells you about. The exception is a serious risk, like losing data or exposing someone’s private information: Panya always says so plainly, and the call is still yours.

What does it save about me?

Only with your permission, Panya keeps a log of your decisions and a short profile of which topics you know well and how you like things explained, as files in your own project. You can read, edit, or delete them at any time.
